# Universidad Nacional (Costa Rica): LLM Knowledge Base

## 1. Purpose and operating rules for the LLM

This document is designed to help an LLM answer practical questions about the **Universidad Nacional of Costa Rica**, normally abbreviated **UNA**. It emphasizes institutional details, student processes, terminology, common ambiguities, and answer-routing rather than generic facts.

The model should follow these rules:

1. **Disambiguate the institution.** “Universidad Nacional” is used by universities in several countries. In a Costa Rican context, “UNA” normally means the public Universidad Nacional headquartered in Heredia, Costa Rica.
2. **Do not confuse UNA with UCR, UNED, TEC, or UTN.**
   - **UNA**: Universidad Nacional.
   - **UCR**: Universidad de Costa Rica.
   - **UNED**: Universidad Estatal a Distancia.
   - **TEC**: Instituto Tecnológico de Costa Rica.
   - **UTN**: Universidad Técnica Nacional.
3. **Preserve Spanish institutional terms.** When answering in English, introduce the Spanish term at first mention because students and official systems use it. Example: “course section reference number (*NRC, Número de Referencia de Curso*)”.
4. **Identify the user’s context before reaching a conclusion.** Material variables include:
   - Admission year.
   - Applicant, first-year, regular, postgraduate, visiting, or international-student status.
   - Campus, regional site, academic unit, and degree.
   - Academic period: cycle, trimester, quadrimester, or summer/third cycle.
   - Scholarship category and benefit type.
   - Whether the question concerns a regulation, a yearly call, or an individual administrative decision.
5. **Separate durable rules from volatile data.**
   - Durable: institutional structure, meanings of major systems, broad admission model, general scholarship categories.
   - Volatile: deadlines, fee amounts, minimum admission scores, course schedules, available spaces, service hours, current authorities, transportation timetables, and annual scholarship requirements.
6. **Never invent a cutoff, vacancy, schedule, fee, current officeholder, email address, or exception.**
7. **Prefer the most recent official source.** When an older FAQ conflicts with a newer regulation, institutional notice, or calendar, use the newer authoritative source and state its date.
8. **Treat individual cases as case-specific.** Scholarship continuity, prerequisite waivers, extraordinary withdrawal, grade appeals, recognition of prior studies, and academic-plan exceptions can depend on documents and a formal decision.
9. **Avoid saying that an application guarantees approval.** This applies to admission, scholarships, residence, benefits, withdrawals, recognition of credits, schedule adjustments, and appeals.
10. **Route the user to the competent unit.** A correct answer often includes both the general rule and the office or system that can issue a binding answer.

## 2. Canonical identity

### 2.1 Core identity

- **Official name:** Universidad Nacional.
- **Common abbreviation:** UNA.
- **Country:** Costa Rica.
- **Institution type:** Public, autonomous university.
- **Headquarters:** Heredia province, principally the Omar Dengo campus.
- **Creation:** Established in 1973 through Costa Rican Law No. 5182.
- **Institutional domain:** `una.ac.cr`.
- **Central telephone:** `+506 2277-3000` as listed in the institutional directory at the time this document was compiled.
- **Official homepage:** https://www.una.ac.cr/
- **Institutional directory:** https://www.directorio.una.ac.cr/

The legal and organizational source of truth is the current **Estatuto Orgánico** and the regulations published through UNA’s official document repository and university gazette.

### 2.2 Identity cues in informal questions

Students may refer to the university as:

- “la UNA”
- “Nacional”
- “la U”
- “Universidad Nacional”
- “UNA Costa Rica”
- A campus name, such as “Omar Dengo,” “Benjamín Núñez,” “Liberia,” or “Pérez Zeledón”

Do not assume that a question mentioning only a campus applies university-wide. Programs, services, schedules, and local contacts can differ by campus.

### 2.3 Institutional mission and culture

UNA commonly frames its work around a humanistic, socially committed public-university mission. Its substantive academic activity is broader than classroom teaching and includes:

- **Docencia:** teaching and learning.
- **Investigación:** research.
- **Extensión:** sustained university engagement with communities and social sectors.
- **Producción académica:** academic and intellectual production.

In UNA terminology, **extensión** should not be translated merely as “extension courses.” It usually refers to community-facing academic action and social engagement. Continuing education is related but not identical.

A career offered at one campus should not be assumed to be offered at another. Even when the degree name is identical, schedules, quotas, local contacts, and admission cutoffs may differ.

## 5. Academic offer and degree terminology

### 5.1 Source of truth for programs

The official program catalog is:

https://www.carreras.una.ac.cr/

Use it to verify:

- Program name.
- Campus or site.
- Academic unit.
- Degree level.
- Study plan.
- Duration.
- Modality.
- Accreditation status.
- Entry conditions.
- Contact details.

Program counts change and should not be hardcoded into long-lived answers.

### 5.2 Costa Rican degree labels commonly encountered at UNA

- **Diplomado:** an undergraduate credential below the bachillerato level in programs that include it.
- **Bachillerato universitario:** the principal undergraduate university degree level; it is not the same as Costa Rican secondary-school *bachillerato*.
- **Licenciatura:** a post-bachillerato professional/academic degree, often involving additional coursework and a graduation requirement.
- **Maestría:** master’s degree.
- **Doctorado:** doctoral degree.
- **Posgrado:** umbrella term for postgraduate programs.

When the user says “bachelor’s,” determine whether they mean:

1. Costa Rican high-school completion, or
2. A university **bachillerato**.

### 5.3 Study plan

The **plan de estudios** determines the required courses, prerequisite sequence, degree requirements, and usually the recommended order.

A student should not rely solely on what appears available in the enrollment system. They should compare:

- Their applicable study-plan version.
- Their academic history.
- Prerequisites and corequisites.
- Course equivalencies or substitutions.
- The current course offering.
- Guidance from the academic unit.

Different cohorts may be governed by different versions of a study plan. A curriculum reform does not automatically mean every current student is moved to the newest plan.

### 5.4 Accreditation

Some programs are accredited by a recognized quality-assurance body, often SINAES. Accreditation status can expire, be renewed, or apply only to a specific campus or degree level. Verify the current program record rather than saying that all UNA programs are accredited.

## 6. Admission to undergraduate programs

### 6.1 Admission is a process, not a single exam result

A useful conceptual sequence is:

1. Register through the public-university admission system.
2. Provide or validate secondary-education information.
3. Pay the applicable fee or receive an exemption.
4. Complete the Prueba de Aptitud Académica (PAA), where applicable.
5. Complete any required specific aptitude test.
6. Obtain an institutional admission average.
7. Participate in career selection and allocation.
8. Be admitted to a particular career/campus based on score, capacity, and rules.
9. Complete title/document registration and enrollment steps.

A user saying “I passed the exam” may only mean they obtained an eligible score. It does **not** necessarily mean they obtained a place in the desired career.

### 6.2 Shared UCR–UNA test does not mean shared admission

UNA and UCR may use a jointly administered PAA. However:

- The universities run separate admission processes.
- Each institution calculates and applies its own admission result and allocation rules.
- Admission to UCR does not automatically confer admission to UNA, and vice versa.
- The same test performance may yield institution-specific outcomes because records, formulas, options, and capacity are applied separately.

Official admission system:
https://admision.ac.cr/

### 6.3 General admission-average model

For recent admission cycles, UNA has used a model in which:

- **60%** is associated with the PAA result.
- **40%** is associated with the secondary-education average.

UNA also describes its model as **estratificado**: contextual variables concerning the applicant’s educational background may be incorporated to promote equitable access. Examples mentioned institutionally include school type or modality, geography, and day/night educational context.

The precise formula and categories can be revised. State the admission year and verify the current official notice or admission regulation before calculating or predicting a result.

### 6.4 Specific aptitude tests

Some artistic careers may require a **Prueba de Aptitud Específica** in addition to the general admission process. Recent calls have included careers associated with:

- Arte Escénico
- Arte y Comunicación Visual
- Enseñanza del Arte y Comunicación Visual
- Danza
- Música

This list and the examination procedure must be checked for the relevant year. Passing a specific aptitude test may be mandatory but still not guarantee a place.

### 6.5 Career allocation, cutoffs, and waiting lists

The **nota de corte** or effective admission cutoff is not a permanent fixed requirement. It can vary by:

- Year.
- Career.
- Campus.
- Quota.
- Applicant demand.
- Admission stage.
- Population or admission route, where rules distinguish them.

Do not use a previous year’s cutoff as a promise. At most, describe it as historical context.

Possible annual stages can include:

- Initial admission/assignment.
- Waiting-list movement.
- Subsequent assignment rounds.
- A process for careers with remaining vacancies.

The exact names and dates vary. Always consult the current admission calendar.

### 6.6 Common admission misconceptions

**Misconception:** “The minimum institutional score is the cutoff for every career.”  
**Correction:** The institutional minimum may make a person eligible to participate, while each career/campus can close at a higher score based on demand and capacity.

**Misconception:** “I passed the UNA/UCR test, so I am enrolled.”  
**Correction:** Admission, career assignment, document registration, and enrollment are separate steps.

**Misconception:** “A career is available everywhere UNA has a campus.”  
**Correction:** Offerings are campus-specific.

**Misconception:** “The admission fee and dates are stable every year.”  
**Correction:** They are annual-call data and must be verified.

### 6.7 Applicants who require special conditions

Admission calls generally provide procedures for applicants who need accessibility accommodations or who belong to populations covered by specific admission policies. The model should avoid guessing what documentation is accepted and route the applicant to the current admission call and the responsible admissions office.

## 7. Registration, enrollment, and the academic lifecycle

### 7.1 Core terms

#### Matrícula

**Matrícula** means the formal enrollment of a student in specific course sections for an academic period. It is not the same as university admission.

#### Empadronamiento

**Empadronamiento** generally refers to the student’s formal association with a degree/program or study plan. It can be particularly relevant in program changes, postgraduate enrollment, re-entry, or administrative records.

#### NRC

**NRC** means **Número de Referencia de Curso**. It identifies a specific course section and is tied to details such as:

- Course code and name.
- Group or section.
- Schedule.
- Instructor.
- Location or delivery arrangement.

Two sections of the same course normally have different NRCs. Entering the wrong NRC can enroll the student in the wrong section.

#### Prerequisite and corequisite

- **Requisito/prerrequisito:** must normally be completed before taking another course.
- **Correquisito:** must normally be taken before or at the same time, according to the study plan.

The enrollment system may enforce these automatically. A technical ability to submit an enrollment request does not itself create an academic exception.

#### Cupo

**Cupo** is available capacity in a course section or program. A course appearing in the schedule does not mean a seat is available.

### 7.2 Academic periods

UNA does not necessarily operate every program on one identical calendar. Official calendars may include:

- **I ciclo**
- **II ciclo**
- **III ciclo** or summer offerings
- Trimesters
- Quadrimesters
- Postgraduate-specific calendars

Use the calendar corresponding to the program and modality. Do not assume that a date for semester-based undergraduate programs applies to every postgraduate program.

### 7.3 Typical enrollment stages

The calendar may distinguish:

- Ordinary online enrollment.
- Online adjustments.
- Administrative adjustments handled through an academic unit.
- Ordinary justified withdrawal.
- Extraordinary justified withdrawal.
- Evaluation of teaching.
- Grade entry and closure of records.

The existence, name, and dates of each stage should be checked in the current academic calendar.

### 7.4 Course conflicts and unavailable courses

When a required course:

- Is full.
- Is not offered.
- Conflicts with another required course.
- Is blocked by an apparent prerequisite error.
- Has an incorrect campus or program restriction.
- Is missing from the student’s plan.

The competent first contact is usually the **academic unit responsible for the student’s degree**, not the LMS support team.

Possible outcomes may include an administrative adjustment, waiting for space, taking a later offering, an authorized substitution, or no exception. The LLM must not promise that a section will be opened or that a prerequisite will be waived.

### 7.5 Withdrawing from a course

Students may encounter forms of **retiro justificado**. The precise consequences depend on:

- Date and withdrawal type.
- Evidence and grounds.
- Academic approval.
- Scholarship/benefit status.
- Whether the course remains on the record and how it is annotated.
- The current academic and scholarship regulations.

A withdrawal accepted for academic purposes does not automatically preserve every scholarship benefit. Scholarship students should check with **Bienestar Estudiantil** before or immediately after changing their course load, especially when receiving a stipend or residence benefit.

### 7.6 Grades and evaluation disputes

The current **Reglamento General del Proceso de Enseñanza y Aprendizaje** is the principal source for issues such as:

- Course syllabus and evaluation plan.
- Attendance.
- Assessment.
- Academic integrity, plagiarism, and copying.
- Grade records.
- Extraordinary or sufficiency examinations.
- Grade challenges and appeals.
- Retention and course repetition.
- Final graduation work.

Do not give a definitive appeal deadline from memory. Retrieve the current regulation and identify the specific procedural article.

Official regulation record:
https://documentos.una.ac.cr/handle/unadocs/14997.6

### 7.7 Academic history and official records

Students may need:

- **Historial académico:** academic history/transcript view.
- **Certificación:** formally issued certified record.
- **Constancia:** official statement of status or facts.
- **Acta de calificaciones:** official grade record for a course/group.
- **Título/diploma:** degree document.
- **Reposición de diploma:** replacement procedure.
- **Reconocimiento/equiparación/convalidación:** formal treatment of prior studies; exact concepts and procedures should be taken from current UNA regulations.

For formal use outside the university, a screenshot from self-service may not substitute for an official certification.

### 7.8 Graduation

Graduation involves more than completing the last class. Depending on the degree, students may need to complete:

- All study-plan requirements.
- Final graduation work or other final requirement.
- Administrative checks.
- Financial or library clearance.
- Graduation application.
- Degree-title registration and ceremony procedures.

Dates and document requirements are published by Registro and may differ by graduation cycle.

## 9. Tuition, fees, and financial terminology

### 9.1 Avoid treating “tuition” as one number

Student charges may involve:

- Cost per credit.
- Other enrollment or welfare charges.
- Laboratory, material, or field-related costs in some contexts.
- Certification and document fees.
- Graduation or diploma-related fees.
- Different rules for scholarships, agreements, or special populations.

The current cost table should be retrieved from Registro or the official student portal. Never quote a historical per-credit value without a year and source.

### 9.2 Exoneration is not the same as a cash scholarship

A student may say “I have a scholarship” when they mean any of the following:

- Partial or full exemption from credit charges.
- Monthly financial support.
- Student residence.
- Student-assistant compensation or exemption.
- Arts, sports, or representation benefit.
- International mobility support.
- Emergency aid.
- Childcare support.

Ask which benefit the student actually has.

### 9.3 Debts and administrative holds

A pending balance or administrative hold may affect enrollment or issuance of services. The model should tell the student to inspect the exact hold in self-service and contact the unit named there. Do not assume every hold is financial.

## 10. Scholarships and student benefits

The principal source is the **Vicerrectoría de Vida Estudiantil**, especially the **Departamento de Bienestar Estudiantil**.

Scholarship hub:
https://www.vidaestudiantil.una.ac.cr/becas

SIBEUNA:
Access through the student portal or official Student Life links.

### 10.1 Socioeconomic scholarship categories

UNA uses socioeconomic categories numbered from **0 to 5**. At the time of compilation, the published credit-charge exemptions were:

| Category | Credit-charge exemption |
|---|---:|
| 0 | 0% |
| 1 | 20% |
| 2 | 40% |
| 3 | 60% |
| 4 | 80% |
| 5 | 100% |

Important interpretation:

- **Category 5 normally means full exemption from credit charges.**
- It does **not**, by itself, mean that the student automatically receives a monthly stipend, residence, food, transport, or every other benefit.
- Separate benefits have separate criteria, applications, capacity, and budget.

### 10.2 Socioeconomic assessment

The category is based on a documented assessment of the student’s and family’s socioeconomic situation. The process may require:

- Application during a defined period.
- Household and income information.
- Supporting documents.
- Corrections or clarifications.
- Verification by Student Life staff.

Submitting a form does not guarantee an award.

### 10.3 Duration and renewal

Published rules have treated the socioeconomic category as having a multi-year validity, commonly up to five years for a first degree, subject to continuing conditions and possible extensions. Specific cases, transfers, licentiate entry, or postgraduate situations may have different periods.

Never conclude that a scholarship “automatically lasts five years.” Check:

- The award resolution.
- Degree level.
- First versus second career.
- Academic progress.
- Annual obligations.
- Current regulation.

### 10.4 Academic-performance requirements

Published scholarship guidance has included requirements such as:

- A minimum annual credit load.
- Passing at least a specified proportion of enrolled credits.
- Maintaining the conditions attached to the particular benefit.

A commonly published socioeconomic-scholarship rule has required at least **18 credits in the previous academic year** and passing at least **80%** of enrolled credits. Failure may reduce the exemption level.

These numbers must be checked against the current scholarship regulation and the student’s population/benefit. Do not apply them mechanically to every postgraduate, disability-related adjustment, authorized reduced load, or special case.

### 10.5 Material changes

Scholarship recipients may be required to report important changes in household, income, residence, or other socioeconomic circumstances. Published guidance has used a period of **30 calendar days** for reporting material changes.

When a student asks whether something must be reported, advise them to contact Bienestar Estudiantil promptly and keep evidence of the communication.

### 10.6 First-career limitation

Many benefits are designed for the student’s first university career. A person who already used benefits for one career may face restrictions in a second career.

Do not state that every second-career student is automatically ineligible. Verify:

- Which benefit was previously used.
- Whether the first credential was completed.
- The current regulatory exception, if any.
- The student’s new degree level.

### 10.7 Results, review, and appeal

Scholarship decisions are normally communicated through official channels, especially institutional email and/or SIBEUNA.

Published scholarship guidance has distinguished:

- A request for review.
- A subsequent appeal.

It has also listed short business-day periods, such as eight business days, for some procedures. Because procedural deadlines are high-stakes, retrieve the current call or regulation and quote the exact article rather than relying on this summary.

### 10.8 Beca Luis Felipe González Flores

This is a distinct benefit, not merely another name for socioeconomic category 5.

It generally provides **monthly financial support** and has been associated with conditions such as:

- Active socioeconomic category 5.
- Enrollment in the full study-plan block or an authorized equivalent.
- Academic-progress requirements.
- Availability of funds.
- Compliance with Student Life follow-up.

Course withdrawal or reduced enrollment can affect the benefit unless formally justified or authorized. A student should consult the assigned social worker before making a load change whenever possible.

### 10.9 Beca Omar Dengo and student residences

The **Beca Omar Dengo** combines a residence component with financial support for eligible students, particularly those with high socioeconomic need who live far from the place of study or face conditions that make daily travel impractical.

UNA has operated student residences at locations including:

- Omar Dengo.
- Benjamín Núñez.
- Pérez Zeledón.
- Sarapiquí.
- Nicoya.
- Liberia.

Capacity is limited. Category 5 does not guarantee a residence place. Rules may cover coexistence, academic load, permitted absences, continued eligibility, and use of facilities.

### 10.10 Beca de Honor

This is a merit-based exemption distinct from socioeconomic aid. Published criteria have included high academic averages and successful completion of a substantial credit load.

Older/currently published thresholds should not be presented without the academic year. Verify:

- Whether the student is new or continuing.
- Required secondary-school or UNA average.
- Required credits.
- Whether 100% of courses must have been passed.
- Maximum duration.

### 10.11 Student assistantships

UNA has several student-assistant categories. Depending on the appointment, an assistantship can provide a credit-charge exemption, financial contribution, or both.

Possible areas include:

- Academic activity.
- Student Life.
- Postgraduate support.
- University Council support.
- Research.
- Extension.
- Teaching support.
- Academic production.
- Academic administration.

Important points:

- Students are normally nominated or selected through an academic/administrative unit.
- Assistantships have defined weekly hours and duties.
- Academic and enrollment requirements apply.
- An assistantship is not necessarily an employment contract.
- Exact compensation, hour limits, and eligibility change by call and category.

### 10.12 Relevant participation scholarships

Benefits may exist for significant participation in:

- University arts groups.
- Sports representation.
- Student government or movement representation.

They can include credit-charge exemption and, in some cases, support for participation expenses. Selection and continued eligibility are governed by the relevant program.

### 10.13 Postgraduate scholarships

UNA offers or administers postgraduate-support schemes, but they are not identical to undergraduate socioeconomic aid. Requirements can include:

- Limited financial resources.
- Full or near-full academic load.
- Passing all courses.
- Not being a UNA employee for a particular benefit.
- Program recommendation.
- Budget availability.

Always check the postgraduate program and current Student Life call.

### 10.14 International mobility support

Financial aid may be available for approved international academic mobility. Requirements can include:

- Formal acceptance or mobility authorization.
- Academic average.
- Study-plan relevance.
- Budget and destination costs.
- Prior institutional approval.

Do not advise a student to buy non-refundable travel before the award and institutional permissions are confirmed.

### 10.15 Humanist or emergency support

UNA has maintained mechanisms such as a **Fondo Humanista** for urgent, exceptional circumstances affecting health, wellbeing, housing, or student continuity.

Emergency support is discretionary and evidence-based. It should not be described as an automatic cash entitlement.

### 10.16 Childcare support

Student Life may offer childcare-related assistance and may coordinate with public services such as CEN-CINAI. Eligibility can depend on:

- Active enrollment.
- Child’s age.
- Socioeconomic need.
- Academic load.
- Available budget and spaces.
- Annual call conditions.

### 10.17 Student loans

Some regulations or legacy pages may mention student loans. Availability can be suspended for a particular year. This is an example of why an LLM must check the current call rather than infer availability from the existence of a regulation.

### 10.18 Scholarship question checklist

Before answering an individual scholarship question, identify:

1. Benefit name.
2. Socioeconomic category.
3. Admission/regular/postgraduate status.
4. First or second career.
5. Credits enrolled and passed.
6. Whether a reduced load was formally authorized.
7. Withdrawal or failure history.
8. Campus and residence status.
9. Date of the event.
10. Current regulation/call and award resolution.

The model should:

- Avoid conducting its own factual adjudication.
- Preserve confidentiality.
- Explain that formal institutional procedures exist.
- Provide current contact information only after verifying the official directory/page.
- Distinguish immediate safety needs from the formal complaint process.

### 12.5 Academic grievances

A complaint about:

- A grade.
- Evaluation method.
- Attendance decision.
- Course syllabus.
- Instructor conduct.
- Academic integrity allegation.

may have a specific escalation chain and deadline in the teaching-and-learning regulation. Retrieve the current rule and identify the competent authority rather than sending every issue directly to the Rector’s Office.

### 12.6 Administrative and regulatory hierarchy

Depending on the issue, authoritative materials can include:

- Costa Rican law.
- Estatuto Orgánico.
- University-wide regulation.
- Council agreement or university gazette.
- Procedure/manual.
- Annual calendar or call.
- Academic-unit decision.
- Individual resolution.

A webpage summary is useful but may not override the current regulation or formal resolution.

## 15. Common student questions and robust answer patterns

This section is written as decision guidance for an LLM.

### 15.1 “What score do I need to enter [career]?”

Answer pattern:

1. Ask/identify the admission year, career, and campus.
2. Explain that the cutoff varies by year and capacity.
3. Distinguish institutional eligibility from actual career assignment.
4. Retrieve the latest official cutoff or allocation result.
5. Label older scores as historical only.

Do not provide one timeless number.

### 15.2 “Can I use my UCR admission exam for UNA?”

Answer pattern:

- Explain that UNA and UCR may share administration of the PAA.
- Clarify that the applicant must be registered for the UNA process and that each university calculates and applies admission independently.
- Verify the current admission call.

### 15.3 “I was admitted. Am I already enrolled?”

Answer pattern:

- No. Admission to a career and course enrollment are separate.
- Check title/document registration, account activation, orientation, and enrollment dates.
- Use the current admitted-student instructions.

### 15.4 “Why can’t I enroll in a course?”

Check:

- Wrong NRC.
- No capacity.
- Time conflict.
- Prerequisite/corequisite.
- Campus/program restriction.
- Hold.
- Wrong study-plan version.
- Enrollment window closed.
- Course not enabled for the student population.
- Technical issue.

Route academic restrictions to the student’s academic unit and technical failures to the support channel identified by the enrollment portal.

### 15.5 “Can they waive a prerequisite?”

Answer pattern:

- Prerequisites are part of the approved study plan.
- Exceptions, if permitted, require formal academic authorization; instructor consent alone may not be sufficient.
- Do not promise approval.
- Contact the academic unit before the adjustment deadline.

### 15.6 “The course I need is not offered. Will I lose my scholarship?”

Answer pattern:

- Do not infer loss automatically.
- Determine the benefit and credit-load rule.
- Ask whether the academic unit can document that the full study-plan block was unavailable.
- Advise the student to seek a formal academic adjustment and consult Bienestar Estudiantil.
- An authorized reduced load can be treated differently from a voluntary reduction, but only the current rule/individual resolution is binding.

### 15.7 “I failed or withdrew from a class. Do I lose category 5?”

Answer pattern:

- Category 5 is an exemption category; continuity depends on current academic-progress requirements and the student’s total annual record.
- One event does not always produce automatic total loss.
- Calculate enrolled and passed credits only after verifying which periods count.
- Check whether the withdrawal is included/excluded under the current regulation.
- Distinguish category reduction from loss of a stipend such as Luis Felipe.
- Route the case to Bienestar Estudiantil.

### 15.8 “Does category 5 include monthly money?”

Answer pattern:

- Category 5 generally provides 100% credit-charge exemption.
- Monthly support is a separate benefit, such as Luis Felipe, and has additional criteria.
- Residence is also a separate benefit.

### 15.9 “Can I receive a scholarship for a second career?”

Answer pattern:

- Many benefits prioritize or are restricted to the first career.
- Ask which previous benefit was used and whether the prior degree was completed.
- Check current exceptions and the individual award record.
- Do not answer solely from the new program’s admission status.

### 15.10 “How do I see my courses online?”

Answer pattern:

- Confirm that the student is officially enrolled in Student Self-Service.
- Identify the correct Aula Virtual instance.
- Check whether the instructor has enabled the course.
- Allow for synchronization, but do not claim a fixed delay unless the current support page states it.
- For a missing course after enrollment is confirmed, contact the instructor/academic unit or LMS support.

### 15.11 “My Moodle password is not my admission password.”

Answer pattern:

- UNA services may have separate authentication contexts or synchronized institutional credentials.
- Confirm the exact URL.
- Use the official password-recovery service from the student portal.
- Never ask the student to send their password.
- Do not recommend automating login or scraping a protected system without reviewing terms, security, and institutional authorization.

### 15.12 “Can I change careers?”

Answer pattern:

- Distinguish internal career change, simultaneous career, new admission, and campus change.
- Requirements can include available capacity, academic record, current admission rules, or a formal application period.
- Scholarship effects must be checked separately.
- Use Registro and the relevant academic units.

### 15.13 “Can I study two careers?”

Answer pattern:

- UNA may have procedures governing simultaneous or second-career enrollment, but acceptance, scheduling, and scholarship coverage are separate issues.
- Confirm the current rule and available spaces.
- Do not assume benefits apply to both programs.

### 15.14 “Can I transfer credits from another university?”

Answer pattern:

- A formal recognition/equivalence process is required.
- Similar course names are not enough.
- Obtain official transcripts and detailed syllabi.
- The academic unit evaluates content and credits; Registro formalizes the result under current procedures.

### 15.15 “Who should I email?”

Use the institutional directory rather than guessing:
https://www.directorio.una.ac.cr/

Ask what the issue is and route it:

- Admission or official academic record → Registro/admissions.
- Course plan, prerequisite, section, graduation requirement → academic unit.
- Scholarship/residence/benefits → Bienestar Estudiantil.
- Counseling/orientation → Orientación y Psicología.
- Medical/dental/nutrition → Salud.
- LMS → Aula Virtual support plus academic unit/instructor.
- Student rights → Defensoría Estudiantil.
- Sexual harassment → Fiscalía contra el Hostigamiento Sexual.
- Library → SIDUNA/local library.
- Student representation → local association or FEUNA.


Retrieval should favor:

1. Current regulation or formal agreement.
2. Current procedure/calendar/call.
3. Current program or office page.
4. Current FAQ.
5. Current institutional news release.
6. Historical material only when explicitly requested.

## 22. Recommended response format for a UNA question

A strong answer normally contains:

1. **Direct answer:** what the general rule means.
2. **Scope:** year, campus, degree, and student/benefit type.
3. **Important distinction:** for example, category versus stipend, admission versus enrollment, LMS versus official enrollment.
4. **Current-source qualification:** state what must be checked.
5. **Action:** name the exact system or competent office.
6. **Evidence:** cite the official page/regulation and its date.
7. **Uncertainty:** state what cannot be determined without the student’s resolution or record.

Example skeleton:

> Under UNA’s general rule, [answer]. In your case, the result depends on [specific variable]. [Important distinction]. Check [system/document] for the [year/period], and request confirmation from [competent unit]. The binding answer is the current regulation and your individual resolution, not a prior-year FAQ.

## 25. Final instruction to an answering LLM

When a question can affect a student’s admission, scholarship, academic record, graduation, money, immigration status, safety, or right to appeal, do not provide a categorical conclusion from this background document alone.

Use this knowledge base to:

- Understand the vocabulary.
- Identify the controlling variables.
- Find the competent office.
- Retrieve the current official rule.
- Explain the distinction clearly.
- Avoid common institutional confusions.

Then ground the answer in the most recent official UNA source and, where applicable, the student’s own formal resolution or record.
